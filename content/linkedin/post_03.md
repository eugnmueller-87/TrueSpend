# Post 3 — Traditional procurement is built on control. There's a better model.
**Publish:** Week 3
**Target reaction:** Intellectual respect. The reader should feel like they've been handed a framework they didn't have before.

---

Traditional procurement is built on control. There's a better model.

Every approval gate exists for the same reason. Without good information, control is the only way to manage risk at scale. You can't trust individual judgment across thousands of transactions when you can't verify whether that judgment was sound. So you don't trust it. You replace it with a process that produces a consistent, defensible outcome regardless of who's involved.

Controls don't create value. They prevent worst outcomes while slowing everything else down and turning procurement into the most resented function in most organisations. That's not an accident. It's what control-based systems do.

The alternative is a confidence model. You don't need to approve every transaction if you're confident the reasoning behind it was correct. You need an audit trail, not an approval chain. You need a confidence score, not a policy checklist. The question stops being "did someone authorise this?" and becomes "was the reasoning sound?"

That shift changes the economics entirely. Most procurement work only exists to manufacture control — the gates, the committees, the documentation, the chasing. Remove the need for control and most of the work disappears. Not because you automated it. Because it only existed as a substitute for something better.

Confidence requires data, reasoning, and traceability. All three are now available at a cost that changes what's possible.
