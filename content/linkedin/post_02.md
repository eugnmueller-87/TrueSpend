# Post 2 — Procurement was never supposed to be a department.
**Publish:** Week 2
**Target reaction:** Recognition. The "yes, exactly" from people who've worked in it and always felt something was off but couldn't name it.

---

Procurement was never supposed to be a department.

It was supposed to answer three questions. Should we buy this? Are our suppliers actually delivering? What leverage do we have in this relationship? Everything that matters in procurement is downstream of those three questions.

Everything else is scaffolding that became the job.

Take a senior procurement person with fifteen years of experience and real commercial instinct. Ask them honestly how much of their week they spend on the three questions. The answer is around 5%. The other 95% is approvals, onboarding forms, status updates, reporting cycles, vendor queries, policy exceptions, and committee prep.

That's not a talent problem. The people I've met in procurement are frequently sharp. It's a systems design problem. The system was built to manufacture control at scale, and it turns out that manufacturing control at scale requires an enormous amount of human effort that has nothing to do with making better commercial decisions.

The three questions still need answering. The question is whether you need a department to answer them, or whether the department was always a workaround for something else.
