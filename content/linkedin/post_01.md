# Post 1 — I'm building something that will make procurement redundant.
**Publish:** Week 1
**Target reaction:** Discomfort. Curiosity. The people who matter will lean in instead of scrolling past.

---

I'm building something that will make procurement redundant.

Not faster. Not smarter. Redundant.

The entire apparatus — approval chains, supplier scorecards, onboarding workflows, category taxonomies, policy checklists — was built to compensate for two things: the absence of good information, and the absence of fast reasoning. Both of those constraints are now gone.

The scaffolding was never the job. It was what you built when you couldn't do the job properly. You couldn't trust a decision without a gate because you didn't have the data to know whether the decision was sound. So you built gates. And then you built teams to manage the gates. And then the gates became the job.

When information is instant and reasoning is cheap, you don't need the scaffolding. You need the judgment that the scaffolding was always trying to approximate.

Most people in procurement will disagree with this. That's fine. The ones who understand it are the ones I want to talk to.
