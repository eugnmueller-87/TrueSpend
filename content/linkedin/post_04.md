# Post 4 — When you remove procurement as a processing function, here's what's left.
**Publish:** Week 4
**Target reaction:** Honest acknowledgment. Not comfort, not cruelty. The people who are good at the real job should feel seen. The people who've been managing process should feel the ground shift slightly.

---

When you remove procurement as a processing function, here's what's left.

Not nothing. Something very different.

Strategic supplier negotiations remain. The conversation with the Dell account director about next year's hardware commercial terms is human, always. It requires relationship, context, judgment about what the other side will move on. No agent closes that conversation — it surfaces everything you need to walk in fully prepared, and then you do the actual work.

Genuine exception handling remains. Novel situations the system hasn't seen before, or situations where the stakes are high enough that human judgment is the appropriate response. The difference is the agent surfaces those exceptions with a full brief, not a half-formed request that someone has to spend two hours investigating before they can make a decision.

Policy ownership remains. Someone has to decide what the system is authorised to do, and those decisions need revisiting as the business changes. That's a new kind of role that doesn't really exist yet.

Maybe 10% of current headcount. But the right 10%. People whose judgment is actually irreplaceable, not people who became very good at managing a process that should never have been human work.

Most procurement professionals are spending their careers on work that shouldn't require a human. That's not their failure. It's the honest description of what the job has become.

---

**Notes:** This post will generate emotional responses. Some defensive, some relieved, some sad. Don't retreat from the honest line at the end — that's the thing people need to hear said plainly. Respond to defensive comments with genuine curiosity: "What part of your current work do you think genuinely requires human judgment?" That's the useful question. Don't be cruel. Be precise.
